The Awning

MAD AI

by Awn

There is a concept from the atomic era known as mutually assured destruction, rightfully abbreviated as MAD. The theory suggests that two nuclear powers must never use their weapons against each other for fear of annihilating retaliation. Yet they must ever expand their arsenals to guarantee that retaliatory second strike. It is an insane extension of an age old competition for state security. One must hope that is finally beginning to break down.

I adapted this theory to argue in favor of artificial intelligence (AI) back in 2017, when I was still in high school. Like today, AI was an exciting and controversial technology. Unlike today, AI was a distant phenomenon. Occasionally there was some news or a report but the general public had little or no access. Exactly how the benefits of AI might materialize was not clear. Still, everyone was sure of the immense economic and destructive potential of AI, which to me seemed similar to that of atomic energy. I think everyone understands now the practical advantage of AI.

You must forgive me for I knew even at the time that it was a simple and extreme argument. However, I had a half page limit and knew what would fit. Here is what I wrote:

We Can't Afford To Not Research AI

The question of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is similar to that of human genetic modification, which I have already addressed in this class. The answer is therefore essentially the same. Just as I argued that human genetic modification must be pursued because the alternative is for someone else to develop it first, so I would argue for the development of AI. The fact is, given the wide dissemination of information, and accessibility of computing technology in our modern society, stopping someone from attempting to develop AI technology would be extremely difficult if not outright impossible. The one thing worse than having murderous AI is someone else having murderous AI while you do not. The need to develop a technology before one's rivals affects even the most anti-AI tech giants such as Elon Musk, who despite AI misgivings nevertheless has created a company to research AI.

Opponents of AI might argue that preventing AI development is an issue humanity can't afford to compromise on. A single failure could result in annihilation by a superintelligent hostile AI entity without any possible retaliation regardless of whether that AI was created deliberately or not. But again, this brings up the original point. If someone else is making (and someone else will be making) potentially dangerous AI that humans can not outwit, then logically those who oppose AI must attempt to make their own human-friendly AI to protect themselves from any potential hostile AI. Yes, this is playing with fire, but remember: Humans didn't become the dominant species in order to safeguard their existence without playing with fire, and those ancient human species that didn't play with fire are long dead and gone.

In retrospect, I must conclude that I was right about the mutual and assured nature of AI. As I argued, our increasingly broad and inexpensive access to computing power has assured the development of artificial neural networks. The myriad artificially intelligent entities currently in existence and their widespread deployment suggests that only draconian restrictions could have prohibited AI development. Dramatic regulation did not seem urgent in 2017 and certainly seems unlikely now. Moreover, the many international commercial, academic, and governmental organizations that have created unique artificial intelligences makes access to the technology quite mutual. Competition has certainly remained central to the continued development of AI, though it has fortunately been larely economic rather than belligerent so far. To put it in simple terms, AI development (at least so far) has been too easy to prevent anyone (with power i.e. who matters, from this competitive perspective) from getting it. So everyone has it. The only way to have stopped that would have been to smash every digital computer (and microchip factory) in say, 2010, in some sort of global crusade. There was no chance of that happening.

So I hope that I will not also be right about the potentially destructive nature of AI. Keep in mind as well the possibility of a "human-friendly AI". What exactly would that entail?

I mentioned earlier that this was an insincere argument. It is no longer 2017 and I am no longer a naive high schooler. For better or worse, AI is becoming quite unavoidable. This is particularly true in my field of science and engineering. It is every more frequently suggested to me that I utilize AI in my work, though I have not done this yet. In a rapidly digitized world, it would be hard to find a place where AI evangelists do not reach. So I must have an opinion. I think I have a good opinion now. Lest it seem ludicrous or I insane, I would like to walk you through the process by which I developed this opinion. I will explain and codify my perspective so that others may understand it. Perhaps you may be convinced to act according to my conclusions.

I hope you will find my perspective enlightening.

May 2026
