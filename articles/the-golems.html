The Awning

The Golems

by Awn

Written into being by the great scholars of our age. Animated by arcane text and incomprehensible formulas. Brought to life and death by mere glyphs on a page. Automatons that might do the tedious and difficult work for us. That can protect us from our existential enemies! Our friends in a world gone mad.

Surely you have heard of the golem? If the stories are to be believed, these were made by the great Rabbis (religious scholars) of medieval Ashkenaz (Europe). They were wrought from clay as God made the first man, Adam, from earth. Made in our image as humanoid automatons to do menial labor. Or, in dire circumstances, formed to protect the Jewish people from profoundly supremacist, racist, and unfortunately popular anti-sematic existential violence. The Rabbis animated them with secret and divine texts in that most ancient and holy language, Hebrew. Then activated them with the single inscribed or inserted word eh-met. Yet golems were always flawed. Perhaps they were not truly intelligent, following instructions literally with disastrous consequences. Perhaps they simply went rogue. One way or another, rightfully or not, fear inspired humans in the vicinity to destroy them. So their creators modified eh-met (truth) to met (death), often suffering serious injury or death themselves in the process.

Of course, I could also be talking about modern artificial intelligence (AI).

Surely most would consider the innovators who contributed to our modern digital neural networks to be great scholars. Many were academics, at least at first, after all. To the layperson, the inner workings of AI are almost totally unknown and unintelligible: basically magic. For that matter, it seems unlikely that any single individual could master AI from the neural network to the firmware to the silicon. Both hardware and software are necessary to make modern AI work even though it may seem that they are merely files full of code. As to their purpose, it is no secret that the principle commercial promise of AI is to deal with the drudgery. Moreover, recent reports suggest that various militaries are also developing AI for security applications.

It is not hard to see the parallels between the ancient myth and our modern reality.

Our fascination with the golem has been documented in myriad similar stories and their derivative works. Think of creation myths with various gods making humans or other creatures out of clay. Or of early modern tales that depict the horrific or farcical reaction to a wide variety of golem analogues. Of science fiction with robots from the nuclear age. Or even of the wide variety of motion pictures, particularly popular around the turn of the century, with artificial intelligence as a central element. When discussing the golem archetype, I may draw on the details of particular golem stories. Worry not if you are unfamiliar with these as the key themes and questions are shared among many golem myth analogues.

The extent to which golem mythologies predict modern concerns about AI is striking. Think of the often implicit criticism of hubris for daring to replicate the act of divine creation. Or the anxiety about and danger in deactivating a dangerous automaton. How could societies that could not possibly imagine actually making an artificially intelligent entity, cultures that predate computers or even industrial technology, anticipate these worries? The similarity between golems and AI suggests that aspects of the human-AI relationship are not new. Stories of golems or robots or the like from the past, especially the distant past, are often criticisms of human behavior. Perhaps the extent of this critique is deeper than anticipated. Who exactly are the golems and what can our mythologies tell us about our new golems?

My path towards understanding this enigma involved particular experiences and events. I will recount these anecdotes next so that you may also comprehend.
